What am I, if I am not the things I create?

AI makes that question harder to leave alone. Writing, coding, the labor of getting an idea out of my head and into something another person can use: I have a lot of myself tied up in being able to do those things. If a tool can do more of the making, I have to ask what I wanted from being the maker.

Some of it is recognition. I want the work to count, and I want the part I did to be seen. There is an ugly possibility in that desire. I could use the tool to make something useful, or use it to produce more things I can point to as evidence of my importance. A faster way to say, look at me.

But I do not think I can settle this by saying the craft was never the point.

That would make a poem worth only the love it won, a book worth only the life it changed. It would leave very little room for writing because I want to write, or making something because I care how it is made. I do not want every hour spent learning to require a beneficiary before I am allowed to value it.

I want to be useful to other people. I also want to be able to do things. Those desires do not always ask the same thing of me. There may be work I should hand to a tool because somebody needs the result more than they need me to have produced it. There may be work I want to do myself because doing it is part of what I want from being alive.

Calling all of that ego would be too easy. So would calling every attachment to my own effort love of craft. I can care about the making and still want far too much credit for having made it.

The question in the title keeps making me choose between being a person and being a maker. I do not know how to pull them apart so cleanly. If I let a tool do the work, what am I giving up? And if I insist on doing it myself, what am I trying to keep?
